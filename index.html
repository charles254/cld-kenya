Who We Are

Rooted in Nairobi, Reaching the Heart of Kenyan Communities

The Center for Livelihood & Development (CLD Kenya) was founded in Nairobi on the conviction that sustainable poverty reduction requires community ownership, climate resilience, and economic independence.

From our Nairobi secretariat in Upper Hill, we coordinate interventions that bridge urban informal settlements like Kibera, Mathare, and Mukuru with arid and semi-arid rural communities in Kitui, Turkana, and Kilifi.

Community-Led

Projects designed and owned by local village and urban ward committees.

High Integrity

Transparent stewardship with audited financials and open public reporting.

Climate Smart

Prioritizing renewable energy, indigenous crops, and water harvesting.

Inclusion First

Targeted empowerment for single mothers, youth, and persons with disabilities.

Eyewitness Field Documentation

The Frontline Reality: Breaking the Cycle of Cattle Rustling

When armed conflict erupts over livestock and depleted pasture, children and community livelihoods suffer first. Here is the raw reality CLD Kenya confronts and resolves on the ground.

The Conflict Trigger

Armed Cattle Rustling & Raids

In the arid valleys of Turkana, Samburu, and Baringo, competition over dwindling dry-season pastures ignites deadly cattle raids. Armed youth clash in dense bush, devastating herd populations and destroying ancestral trade corridors.

CLD Field Metric: Over 65% of regional violence originates from unmediated livestock theft and water pan disputes.
Rebuilding Education

Desks Over Disruption: Restoring Schools

When cattle rustling forces schools to close, children lose years of critical learning. Through CLD's negotiated demilitarized school peace buffer zones, closed learning grounds are reopened and protected. Young girls and boys sit safely at wooden desks with books, replacing fear with hope.

CLD Field Metric: 34 schools reopened with demilitarized buffer zones and 4,200+ pupils returned to formal classes.
The Sustainable Remedy

Women & Elder Peace Circles

Real peace is born in the community sand circles. CLD mobilizes pastoralist mothers, clan matriarchs, and traditional elders into binding dialogue councils to negotiate ceasefires, recover stolen livestock, and open safe trade routes.

Peace Accord Dividend: 48 binding inter-clan treaties brokered with a 94.2% verified 24-month ceasefire compliance rate.
