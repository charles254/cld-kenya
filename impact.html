Suguta Valley • Cross-County Elder Peace Accord

Silencing Guns, Opening Corridors: The Suguta Valley Peace Treaty

Decades of cyclic cattle rustling and dry-season pasture clashes once made the border between Turkana and Samburu impassable. CLD deployed accredited Alternative Dispute Resolution (ADR) mediators to host a series of neutral Lomerani elder barazas.

The resulting peace accord codified reciprocal grazing rights and established a shared solar water trough. Today, morans from both communities jointly manage the livestock dipping stations and trade peacefully at cross-border markets.

"Before CLD convened our council under the acacia tree, our boys carried rifles. Today, our children herd livestock side-by-side without fear." — Mzee Ekeno, Peace Council Elder
Nairobi Informal Settlements • Youth De-radicalization

From Street Warfare to Tech Micro-Enterprises in Mathare

In the dense alleys of Mathare, territorial turf battles between rival youth gangs often disrupted informal markets and subjected young men to violence. CLD peace facilitators conducted confidential peer dialogues to broker a mutual non-aggression truce.

As a direct peace dividend, former rivals were co-enrolled in CLD's Eastlands Youth Digital & Agribusiness Hub. Over 280 ex-combatants have graduated with certified digital livelihoods, operating smartphone repair and vertical sack-farming collectives.

"Mediation showed us that poverty was our real enemy, not our brothers on the opposite street. Today we build apps and run chicken coops together." — Kevin O., Youth Peace Envoy
Turkana Central • Women-Led Shared Water Peace Kiosk

Women as Peace Brokers: Ending Border Clashes Around Water Pans

When scarce dry-season water points triggered recurring ethnic skirmishes in Turkana Central, women bore the brunt of both violence and 5-hour daily water treks. Under CLD's UNSCR 1325 Women Mediators network, local mothers stepped up to lead joint inter-community dialogues.

CLD supported their treaty by drilling a 140m solar borehole at a neutral midpoint. Co-managed by a bi-clan women's committee, the kiosk provides 25,000 liters of safe water daily and irrigates a joint organic vegetable cooperative.

"Women brought the clans together when politicians failed. Our children drink the same clean water, and our market stalls thrive." — Akiru, Women Peace Committee Chair
