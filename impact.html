Suguta & North Rift • Cattle Rustling & ADR Peace Accord

Silencing Guns, Ending Raids: The Suguta Valley Peace Treaty

Decades of cyclic cattle rustling and dry-season pasture clashes once made the border between Turkana, Samburu, and Baringo impassable. Armed youth frequently raided livestock in dense bush, leaving devastation and inter-generational bitterness. CLD deployed accredited Alternative Dispute Resolution (ADR) mediators to host a series of neutral Lomerani elder barazas.

The resulting peace accord codified reciprocal grazing rights, facilitated the return of raided livestock, and established a shared solar water trough. Today, morans from previously warring communities jointly manage livestock dipping stations and trade peacefully at cross-border markets.

"Before CLD convened our council under the acacia tree, our boys carried rifles and raided across borders. Today, our young herders trade side-by-side in peace." — Mzee Ekeno, Peace Council Elder
ASAL Corridors • Customary Council of Elders Mediation

Under the Acacia Tree: Restoring Peace Through Grassroots Barazas

When livestock raids and resource disputes erupt, formal justice systems are often hundreds of miles away. True resolution occurs when traditional elders, community chiefs, and clan matriarchs gather in the shade of the sacred acacia tree. CLD equips local peace committees with Alternative Dispute Resolution (ADR) frameworks to arbitrate customary compensation and seal binding non-aggression treaties.

Through these inclusive barazas, warring communities have demobilized armed morans, returned hundreds of stolen cattle without bloodshed, and opened vital trade roads between Turkana, West Pokot, and Baringo.

"The shadow of the acacia tree is where our ancestors forgave. CLD brought our communities back to this table, proving that peaceful words are stronger than bullets." — Senior Elder Lokor, Peace Council Co-Chair
Northern Kenya • Women-Led Peace Barazas (UNSCR 1325)

Mothers in the Sand: How Women Peace Circles Brokered Border Truces

When men went to war over dried water pans, pastoralist women bore the heaviest burden. In traditional beadwork circles gathered on the river sand, CLD mobilized mothers from opposing clans to form joint Women Peace Monitoring Committees under the UN Resolution 1325 framework.

These courageous women used informal intelligence networks to warn elders before retaliatory cattle raids occurred, successfully disarming young fighters through familial persuasion and opening neutral women's marketplaces.

"When politicians failed, we sat together on the sand as mothers. A bullet does not choose whose child it kills. We chose peace for our children." — Akiru, Women Peace Committee Chair
Conflict Corridors • Reclaiming Classrooms & Education

Desks Over Disruption: Returning Children Safely to School

Whenever cattle rustlers struck, schools were abruptly deserted and children fled for their lives. CLD's inter-clan peace pacts specifically established "Demilitarized School Peace Zones", declaring learning grounds neutral sanctuaries monitored by joint community guardians.

With safe corridors restored, children have returned from displacement back into formal classrooms. Equipped with school supplies, learning desks, and peace curricula, young pastoralist girls and boys are reading books together, replacing the trauma of conflict with the promise of education.

"Our children used to scatter into the bushes when gunfire echoed. Now that our elders made peace, they sit safely at their desks with open books and bright dreams." — Lokidor, Headteacher & Community Peace Liaison
